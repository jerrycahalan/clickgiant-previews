Hurt at work? Here's how it's different

A workplace injury isn't handled like a car wreck. Illinois workers' compensation is a separate system with its own rules. In general, benefits are paid regardless of who caused the accident, so you don't have to prove your employer did anything wrong. The trade-off is that the claim goes before the Illinois Workers' Compensation Commission, where an arbitrator hears the case, not a jury in a regular courtroom.

Step by step

What to do after a work injury in Illinois

A few early steps protect your benefits.

  1. Get medical care right away.Tell the doctor or ER that you were hurt at work, so it's in your records from the start.
  2. Tell your employer in writing.Report the accident as soon as you can. Illinois law requires notice no later than 45 days after the accident. Keep a copy of what you sent.
  3. Write down what happened.Note the date, time, place, what you were doing, and the names of anyone who saw it.
  4. Keep every record.Save medical bills, doctor's notes, work restrictions, pay stubs and anything the insurance company sends you.
  5. Follow your doctor's restrictions.If you're released to light duty, keep the paperwork. Benefits often turn on what your doctor says you can and can't do.
  6. Don't wait on a denial.If your claim is denied, your checks stop or a settlement is offered, talk to a lawyer before you sign anything.
  7. Call us for a free consultation.We'll listen, explain your options and connect you with the right workers' comp attorney. There's no cost to talk.
What comp can cover

Benefits available after a work injury

Medical care

Treatment reasonably necessary to cure or relieve the effects of your injury.

Temporary total disability

Checks while your doctor keeps you off work, generally two-thirds of your average weekly wage, subject to state minimums and maximums.

Temporary partial disability

Help when you're back on light duty but earning less than before.

Permanent disability

Compensation for a lasting injury or disfigurement, including the loss of use of a hand, arm, leg or other body part.

Vocational rehabilitation

Training and job-search help if you can't return to your old job.

Death benefits

Benefits for a worker's family when a job injury is fatal.

Straight answers

Workers' comp FAQ

Do I have to prove my employer was at fault?

Generally, no. Illinois workers' compensation benefits are paid regardless of fault. You do have to show the injury arose out of and in the course of your job.

Can I be fired for filing a workers' comp claim?

It's illegal for an employer to fire, harass, refuse to rehire or discriminate against you for exercising your workers' comp rights. If that happens, you may have a separate claim in court.

Can I sue my employer?

In most cases workers' comp is your remedy against your employer, not a lawsuit. But if someone else caused your injury, such as another driver, a contractor or the maker of a defective machine, you may also have an injury claim against them. Trent/Michael to confirm

Who decides my case if there's a dispute?

An arbitrator of the Illinois Workers' Compensation Commission holds the hearing. Either side can ask a panel of three commissioners to review the decision, and most cases can then be appealed to circuit court.

How much does a workers' comp lawyer cost?

Nothing up front. Attorney fees in Illinois workers' comp cases are limited by law, generally to 20% of the compensation recovered, unless the Commission approves more after a hearing.

Workers' comp is a specialized system with its own hearings and rules. Partnering with an experienced comp attorney gets you the right lawyer for that claim, while we stay involved and handle any related injury case.
