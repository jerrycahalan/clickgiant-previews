Step by step

What to do after a motorcycle accident

Your health comes first. Then protect your claim.

  1. Call 911 and get checked out.Let paramedics look at you even if you think you're fine. Adrenaline hides injuries, and head injuries can show up later.
  2. Keep your gear.Don't throw away your helmet, jacket or boots. Damage to your gear helps show how the crash happened and how hard you hit.
  3. Take photos.Photograph the bike, the other vehicle, the road, skid marks, debris and your injuries, including road rash.
  4. Get names and insurance.Get the driver's name, phone, insurance company and policy number, plus anyone who saw the crash.
  5. Don't fix the bike yet.Let us document the damage before anything is repaired or sold for parts.
  6. Don't give a recorded statement.The other insurer may try to get you to say you were going too fast. Talk to a lawyer first.
  7. Call us for a free consultation.We'll tell you what your case may be worth and what happens next. No cost, no obligation.

Know your rights

Illinois laws that affect motorcycle cases

2 years

To file an injury claim

Most motorcycle injury claims must be filed within 2 years of the crash. Claims against a town, county or other local government have a shorter deadline.

Source735 ILCS 5/13-202Illinois Code of Civil Procedure ↗
50% rule

Shared fault

Insurers love to blame the rider. You can still recover as long as you were 50% or less at fault, and your award is reduced by your share.

Source735 ILCS 5/2-1116Illinois Code of Civil Procedure ↗
Eyes covered

Eye protection

Illinois requires motorcycle operators and passengers to wear glasses or goggles, or ride behind a windshield that extends above the eyes. Contact lenses don't count.

Source625 ILCS 5/11-1404Illinois Vehicle Code ↗
Who pays your bills

Where the money comes from after a motorcycle crash

Riders often have fewer coverage options than drivers think, so we look for every policy that applies.

  • The at-fault driver's insurance. Their liability coverage pays for your injuries, lost wages and pain and suffering.
  • Your uninsured or underinsured motorist coverage. It steps in when the driver has no insurance or not enough for a serious injury.
  • Your motorcycle policy's medical payments coverage. If you have it, it can help with early medical bills.
  • Your health insurance. It can cover treatment now, and we sort out repayment when the case settles.
  • Employers and businesses. If the driver was working, their employer may also be responsible.
Straight answers

Motorcycle accident FAQ

Can I still recover if I wasn't wearing a helmet?

In many cases, yes. Not wearing a helmet doesn't cause a crash. The other side may argue it made some injuries worse, and we deal with that argument with the medical evidence. Trent/Michael to confirm

The insurance company says I was speeding. What now?

That's one of the most common tactics in motorcycle cases. We look at the crash report, the damage, the scene, witnesses and any video to show what really happened. Even if you were partly at fault, you can recover as long as you were 50% or less to blame.

What if the driver says they didn't see me?

"I didn't see the motorcycle" isn't a defense. Drivers have a duty to look for everyone on the road, including riders.

Will my motorcycle insurance go up if I file a claim?

A claim against the at-fault driver's insurance generally shouldn't count against you. Ask your own agent about your policy, and we can help you understand your options.

Can a passenger file a claim?

Yes. Passengers can make claims against the driver who caused the crash, and sometimes against the rider's policy too.

How much does it cost to hire you?

Nothing up front. We work on a contingency fee and are only paid if we recover money for you. If an insurer makes an offer before you hire us, we don't take a fee from that money.
