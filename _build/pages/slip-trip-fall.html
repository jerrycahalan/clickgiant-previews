Hurt in a fall that never should have happened?

A fall can break a hip, a wrist or an ankle, injure your back or cause a head injury. For older adults, one fall can change everything. And the property owner's insurance company will often try to blame you for not watching where you were going.

Illinois property owners have to use reasonable care to keep their property safe for visitors. When a store, restaurant, landlord or business ignores a spill, a broken step, torn carpet, an uneven walkway or bad lighting, and you get hurt, they can be held responsible. What to do after a slip, trip or fall in Illinois

The hazard that hurt you can be fixed or cleaned up within hours. Here's what protects you most.

  1. Get medical care.Let someone help you, call 911 if you need to, and get checked out. Hip, back and head injuries can be worse than they first feel.
  2. Report it before you leave.Tell the manager, owner or landlord what happened and ask for a copy of the incident report, or at least the name of the person who took it.
  3. Photograph the hazard.Get the spill, the broken step, the ice or the uneven pavement from several angles before anyone fixes it. Include any missing warning signs.
  4. Get witness names.Anyone who saw you fall, or who saw the hazard before you did, can help prove the owner should have known about it.
  5. See a doctor ASAP.Gaps in treatment are the first thing insurers use to argue you weren't really hurt. Follow your doctor's plan and keep every appointment.
  6. Keep your shoes and clothes.Don't wash or throw them away. Insurers often blame footwear, and your shoes can prove them wrong.
  7. Call us quickly about cameras.Many businesses record over security video within days. We send a letter demanding they keep it.

Illinois laws that affect a slip, trip or fall case

2 years

To file an injury claim

Most fall injury claims must be filed within 2 years of the fall.

Source735 ILCS 5/13-202Illinois Code of Civil Procedure ↗
Reasonable care

The property owner's duty

Illinois property owners owe people lawfully on their property reasonable care under the circumstances. The old rules treating some visitors differently were abolished.

Source740 ILCS 130/2Illinois Premises Liability Act ↗
1 year

Falls on public property

Claims against a town, county or other local government must be filed within 1 year. The government generally must have known, or should have known, about the hazard in time to fix it.

Who pays your bills

Where the money comes from after a fall

Fall claims are paid by insurance, not out of someone's pocket. Part of our job is finding every policy that applies.

  • The business's liability insurance. Stores, restaurants and other businesses carry coverage for injuries on their property.
  • The landlord's or property owner's insurance. Owners are responsible for hazards in areas they control, like stairs, hallways and parking lots.
  • Medical payments coverage. Many property policies include it, and it can pay some medical bills no matter who was at fault.
  • Maintenance and snow removal companies. If a contractor was hired to clean, repair or clear the property, it may share responsibility.
  • Your health insurance. It can cover treatment now, and we sort out repayment when the case settles.
Straight answers

Slip, trip & fall FAQ

Do I have a case just because I fell?

Not every fall is someone else's fault. You have a case when the owner knew, or should have known, about a dangerous condition and didn't fix it or warn you, and that condition caused your fall. We'll tell you honestly whether that fits your situation.

The insurance company says I should have seen it. Is that the end of my case?

Not necessarily. Owners and insurers often argue a hazard was "open and obvious." But if something distracted you, or you had no reasonable choice but to walk through it, the owner may still be responsible.

I slipped on ice. Can I make a claim?

It depends on how the ice got there. Illinois property owners usually aren't responsible for ice and snow that builds up naturally. They can be responsible when they caused an unnatural build-up, like a leaking gutter or a downspout draining across a walkway, or made it worse with careless snow removal.

I fell on a city sidewalk. What should I know?

Claims against a town, county or other local government have a shorter, 1-year deadline and special rules about whether the government knew about the hazard. Call us as soon as you can.

What if I was partly at fault?

You can still recover in Illinois as long as you were 50% or less at fault. Your compensation is reduced by your share of the blame.
