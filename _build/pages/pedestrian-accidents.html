Step by step

What to do after you're hit by a car

If you can't do these yourself, ask a family member to help.

  1. Call 911 and get medical care.Report the crash so police create a record, and get checked out right away.
  2. Get the driver's information.Name, phone, license plate, insurance company and policy number.
  3. Find witnesses and cameras.Get names and numbers of anyone who saw it. Nearby homes and businesses may have video, but it's often erased within days.
  4. Take photos.The scene, the crosswalk or roadside, the vehicle, lighting and your injuries.
  5. Keep your clothes and shoes.Don't wash or throw them away. They can be important evidence.
  6. Don't talk to the driver's insurer.Don't give a recorded statement or sign anything before you talk to a lawyer.
  7. Call us for a free consultation.We'll explain your options and the deadlines that apply. No cost, no obligation.

Know your rights

Illinois laws that protect pedestrians

Stop & yield

Crosswalks

Where there's no traffic signal working, a driver must stop and yield to a pedestrian crossing in a crosswalk. Drivers behind a car that has stopped for a pedestrian may not pass it.

Source625 ILCS 5/11-1002Illinois Vehicle Code ↗
2 years

To file an injury claim

Most pedestrian injury claims must be filed within 2 years of the crash. Claims against a town, county or other local government have a shorter deadline.

Source735 ILCS 5/13-202Illinois Code of Civil Procedure ↗
50% rule

Shared fault

Even if the driver says you stepped out suddenly or weren't in a crosswalk, you can still recover as long as you were 50% or less at fault.

Source735 ILCS 5/2-1116Illinois Code of Civil Procedure ↗
Who pays your bills

Where the money comes from after a pedestrian crash

Many people are surprised to learn their own car insurance can help even though they were walking.

  • The driver's insurance. Their liability coverage pays for your injuries, lost wages and pain and suffering.
  • Your own auto policy. Uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage may apply when the driver has little or no insurance, or drives off. Confirm UM wording
  • A household member's policy. If you don't own a car, a relative's policy you live with may cover you. Trent/Michael to confirm
  • Your health insurance. It can cover treatment now, and we sort out repayment when the case settles.
  • Employers and businesses. If the driver was working, their employer may also be responsible.
Straight answers

Pedestrian accident FAQ

What if I wasn't in a crosswalk?

You may still have a case. Drivers have a duty to watch for people on foot everywhere, not just in crosswalks. Where you were crossing can affect how fault is shared, and you can recover as long as you were 50% or less at fault.

The driver drove off. Can I still recover?

Often, yes. Report it to police right away. Uninsured motorist coverage on your own policy, or a household member's policy, may pay for your injuries.

Who pays my medical bills right now?

Your health insurance, and any medical payments coverage you have, can pay bills as they come in. We sort out repayment when the case settles.

My child was hit by a car. What should I do?

Get them medical care first, then call us. Children's cases have special rules about deadlines and settlements, and we'll walk you through them.

How much is my case worth?

It depends on your injuries, medical bills, lost wages, how your life has changed and how much insurance is available. We'll give you an honest range at your free consultation.

How much does it cost to hire you?

Nothing up front. We work on a contingency fee and are only paid if we recover money for you. If an insurer makes an offer before you hire us, we don't take a fee from that money.
