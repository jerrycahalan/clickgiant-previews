Step by step

What to do after a defective product injury

What you do with the product matters as much as anything.

  1. Get medical care.Tell your doctor exactly how you were hurt and which product was involved.
  2. Keep the product and its packaging.Don't throw it away, repair it or send it back to the company. Store it somewhere safe, along with manuals, receipts and boxes.
  3. Take photos.Photograph the product, the scene, any labels or serial numbers, and your injuries.
  4. Check for recalls.Look up vehicle recalls by VIN at NHTSA, consumer product recalls at CPSC, and drug and device safety alerts at FDA.
  5. Report the problem.You can report unsafe consumer products on SaferProducts.gov and drug or device problems through the FDA's MedWatch program.
  6. Call us for a free consultation.We'll review what happened, explain your options and help preserve the evidence.

Straight answers

Product liability FAQ

What makes a product "defective"?

Generally, a product may be defective if it was designed in a dangerous way, was made wrong compared to the rest of the line, or was sold without adequate instructions or warnings about its risks. Trent/Michael to confirm

Do I have to prove the company was careless?

Not always. Product cases can focus on whether the product itself was unreasonably dangerous, not just on what the company did. We'll explain how that works for your case. Trent/Michael to confirm

What if the product was recalled?

A recall can help show there was a problem, but you can have a case without one, and a recall doesn't automatically mean you do. Keep the product either way.

Should I send the product back to the manufacturer?

No. Keep it, along with the packaging and receipts. The product is usually the most important evidence in the case.

How long do I have to file?

Most Illinois injury claims must be filed within 2 years, and product cases can have other time limits too. Call as soon as you can.
