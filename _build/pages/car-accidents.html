Step by step

What to do after a car accident in Illinois

The first few days decide a lot about your case. Here's what protects you most.

  1. Call 911 and get checked out.Report the crash so police create a record, and let paramedics look at you even if you feel fine. Neck, back and head injuries often show up hours or days later.
  2. Take photos before anything moves.Get every vehicle, the damage, skid marks, traffic signs, the road conditions and your injuries. Your phone is the best evidence-gathering tool you have.
  3. Swap information and find witnesses.Get the other driver's name, phone, insurance company and policy number, plus names and numbers of anyone who saw it happen.
  4. Get the crash report number.Ask the officer, whether it's local police, the county sheriff or the Illinois State Police, how to get a copy of the report.
  5. See a doctor within a few days.Gaps in treatment are the first thing insurers use to argue you weren't really hurt. Follow your doctor's plan and keep every appointment.
  6. Don't give the other insurer a recorded statement.Their adjuster may sound friendly, but their job is to pay as little as possible. Don't sign anything or accept a quick offer before you talk to a lawyer.
  7. Call us for a free consultation.We'll tell you what your case may be worth, what deadlines apply and what happens next. There's no cost and no obligation.

Know your rights

Illinois car accident laws that affect your case

2 years

To file an injury claim

Most car accident injury claims must be filed within 2 years of the crash. Claims against a town, county or other local government have a shorter, 1-year deadline.

Source735 ILCS 5/13-202Illinois Code of Civil Procedure ↗
50% rule

Shared fault

If you were partly to blame, you can still recover as long as you were 50% or less at fault. Your compensation is reduced by your share of the fault.

Source735 ILCS 5/2-1116Illinois Code of Civil Procedure ↗
25/50 minimum

Required insurance

Illinois drivers must carry at least $25,000 per person and $50,000 per crash for injuries. Serious injuries often exceed those limits, which is why your own coverage matters too.

Source625 ILCS 5/7-203Illinois Vehicle Code ↗
Who pays your bills

Where the money comes from after a crash

Most people don't realize how many insurance policies can apply to one accident. Part of our job is finding every one of them.

  • The at-fault driver's insurance. Their liability coverage pays for your injuries, lost wages and pain and suffering.
  • Your uninsured or underinsured motorist coverage. It steps in when the other driver has no insurance or not enough.
  • Medical payments coverage. If you have it, it can pay early medical bills no matter who caused the crash.
  • Your health insurance. It can cover treatment now, and we sort out repayment when the case settles.
  • Employers and businesses. If the other driver was working, their employer may also be responsible.
Straight answers

Car accident FAQ

How much is my car accident case worth?

It depends on your injuries, your medical bills (now and in the future), your lost wages, how the crash has changed your daily life, and how much insurance is available. We'll give you an honest range at your free consultation once we understand your injuries and treatment.

Should I talk to the other driver's insurance company?

Report the crash to your own insurer, but be careful with the other driver's company. Don't give a recorded statement or sign a medical release until you've talked to a lawyer. Adjusters use those statements to limit what they pay.

What if the other driver didn't have insurance?

Illinois requires drivers to carry uninsured motorist coverage, so your own policy can pay for your injuries. The same coverage often applies to hit-and-run crashes. We handle these claims against your own insurer, too.

What if I was partly at fault?

You can still recover in Illinois as long as you were 50% or less at fault. Your compensation is reduced by your share of the blame. Insurers often exaggerate a driver's fault to pay less, and we push back with the evidence.

Do I need a lawyer for a minor accident?

Not always. If you weren't hurt and only your car was damaged, you may be able to handle it yourself. If you were injured, missed work or are still in treatment, talk to a lawyer before accepting any offer. The consultation is free.

How much does it cost to hire you?

Nothing up front. We work on a contingency fee and are only paid if we recover money for you. If an insurer makes an offer before or just as you hire us, we don't take a fee from that money.
