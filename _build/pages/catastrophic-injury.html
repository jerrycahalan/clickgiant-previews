The long view

What your case should cover

Catastrophic injury cases are about the future as much as the past. We make sure the claim includes all of it.

  • Medical care, now and later. Surgeries, therapy, medication, equipment and care you'll need years from now.
  • Lost income and earning power. Not just missed paychecks, but the work you may not be able to do anymore.
  • Changes to your home and vehicle. Ramps, wider doors, accessible bathrooms and hand controls.
  • Help at home. In-home care and the time family members spend caring for you.
  • Pain, suffering and loss of a normal life. The things you can't do anymore matter, too.
Know your rights

Illinois laws that affect catastrophic injury cases

2 years

To file an injury claim

Most personal injury claims must be filed within 2 years of the injury. Claims against a town, county or other local government have a shorter deadline.

Source735 ILCS 5/13-202Illinois Code of Civil Procedure ↗
50% rule

Shared fault

You can still recover as long as you were 50% or less at fault. Your compensation is reduced by your share of the fault.

Source735 ILCS 5/2-1116Illinois Code of Civil Procedure ↗
25/50 minimum

Insurance limits

Illinois drivers only have to carry $25,000 per person in injury coverage. Serious injuries far exceed that, so we look for every other policy that can pay.

Source625 ILCS 5/7-203Illinois Vehicle Code ↗
Step by step

What to do after a serious injury

Many of these steps can be handled by a family member.

  1. Follow your doctors' plan.Keep every appointment and therapy session. It protects your health and your case.
  2. Keep a journal.Write down pain, limits and what you can't do anymore. Family members can help.
  3. Save every bill and receipt.Medical bills, mileage to appointments, equipment and help you've paid for.

Straight answers

Catastrophic injury FAQ

What counts as a catastrophic injury?

Generally, an injury that permanently changes your ability to work or live the way you did before, like a spinal cord or brain injury, amputation or severe burns.

How do you figure out what my future care will cost?

We work with your doctors and, when needed, with experts who plan future care and calculate lost earning power, so the claim reflects your real needs over time.

What if the at-fault driver's insurance isn't enough?

That's common in serious injury cases. We look for other policies, such as your underinsured motorist coverage, employer policies and other people or companies who share the blame.

Can you come to the hospital or my home?

Yes. We come to you, whether that's your home, the hospital or a rehab center.

Should I accept the insurance company's offer?

Not before talking to a lawyer. Once you sign a release you can't go back, and early offers rarely account for future care.

How much does it cost to hire you?

Nothing up front. We work on a contingency fee and are only paid if we recover money for you. If an insurer makes an offer before you hire us, we don't take a fee from that money.
