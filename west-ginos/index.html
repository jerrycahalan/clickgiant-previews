Illinois injury law, plainly

What is personal injury law?

If someone else's carelessness hurt you, Illinois law lets you hold them responsible for your medical bills, lost pay and pain. Most cases come down to negligence. The other side owed you reasonable care, didn't give it, and that caused your injuries.

In practice, you're usually dealing with an insurance company, not the person who hurt you. Our job is to prove what happened and show what it cost you, then make the insurer pay a fair amount.

See the cases we handle →

The four things we have to prove

1. Duty. The other driver, owner or company owed you reasonable care.

2. Breach. They didn't exercise reasonable care. For example, they ran a stop sign or ignored a trip hazard.

3. Cause. That failure caused your injury.

4. Damages. You lost something real: health, wages and time.

What your claim can cover

What compensation is available in Illinois?

Illinois lets you recover both the money an injury costs you and the harm that doesn't come with a receipt.

Economic damages

  • Medical bills, now and in the future
  • Lost wages and benefits
  • Reduced ability to earn a living
  • Rehab, therapy and in-home care
  • Vehicle and property damage
  • Out-of-pocket costs such as mileage to appointments

Non-economic damages

  • Pain and suffering
  • Emotional distress
  • Loss of a normal life
  • Disfigurement
  • Loss of companionship (for spouses and families)
Straight answers

Personal injury FAQ

How much does it cost to hire the Law Offices of West & Ginos, LLC?

Nothing up front. We work on a contingency fee, so we're paid a percentage of what we recover for you. If we don't recover money, you don't owe us a fee.

If an insurer makes an offer before or just as you hire us, we don't take a fee from that offer.

What if the other side says I was partly at fault?

Illinois uses modified comparative fault. You can still recover if you were 50% or less at fault, but your award is reduced by your share. At more than 50%, you can't recover. Insurers use this to shift blame, and we push back with evidence.

How long will my case take?

Many cases settle within several months once treatment is finished and the claim is fully valued. Cases that go to suit take longer, often a year or more. We'll give you a realistic timeline at the first meeting and keep you updated as it changes.

Do I have to drive to your office?

No. We have offices in Litchfield and Nokomis, and we're glad to meet you at home or in the hospital, or talk by phone or video.

What should I do right after a car accident?

Get medical care, take photos, collect the other driver's insurance and any witness names, and get the crash report number. Don't give a recorded statement to the other driver's insurer before you talk to a lawyer.
