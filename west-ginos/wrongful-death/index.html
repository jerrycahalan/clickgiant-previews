Know your rights

How Illinois wrongful death claims work

2 years

Deadline to file

A wrongful death case usually must be filed within 2 years of the death. The law allows more time in some cases, such as deaths caused by violent intentional acts, and for minors.

Source740 ILCS 180/2Illinois Wrongful Death Act ↗
Family

Who the claim is for

The case is brought by the personal representative of the estate, for the benefit of the surviving spouse and next of kin. Money is divided based on each person's dependency on the person who died.

Source740 ILCS 180/2Illinois Wrongful Death Act ↗
Grief

What the family can recover

A jury can award the family's financial losses, including grief, sorrow and mental suffering. Punitive damages may be available in some cases, but not against a government or in medical or legal malpractice cases.

Source740 ILCS 180/2Illinois Wrongful Death Act ↗

A separate claim may also be brought for what your loved one went through before passing, such as medical bills and pain and suffering. Confirm survival claim wording

What to do after losing a loved one

There's no rush on most of this. Take care of your family first.

  1. Take the time you need.Funeral plans and family come first. Just don't wait so long that evidence is lost or the deadline gets close.
  2. Keep important papers.Death certificate, crash report, medical bills, funeral bills, pay stubs and tax returns.
  3. Don't talk to the other side's insurer.Don't give a recorded statement or sign anything before you talk to a lawyer.

Straight answers

Wrongful death FAQ

Who can file a wrongful death claim in Illinois?

The claim is filed by the personal representative of the estate, usually the executor named in a will or an administrator appointed by the court. It's brought for the benefit of the surviving spouse and next of kin. We help families set this up.

How long do we have to file?

Usually 2 years from the date of death. Some situations allow more time, but it's best to talk to a lawyer early so evidence isn't lost.

What damages can the family recover?

The family's financial losses, such as lost income and support, plus damages for grief, sorrow and mental suffering. Punitive damages may be available in some cases.

How is the money divided?

Under Illinois law it's divided among the spouse and next of kin based on how much each depended on the person who died. The court approves the split.

What if there's also a criminal case?

A wrongful death claim is separate from any criminal case against the driver. You can bring a civil claim whether or not there are criminal charges, and the deadline may be extended in some criminal cases.

How much does it cost?

Nothing up front. We work on a contingency fee, so there's no fee unless we recover money for your family.
