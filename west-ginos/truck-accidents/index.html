Step by step

What to do after a truck accident in Illinois

Truck cases are won or lost on evidence, and much of it is in the trucking company's hands. Here's what protects you most.

  1. Call 911 and get checked out.Report the crash and let paramedics look at you, even if you feel fine. Injuries from a truck crash are often worse than they first seem.
  2. Photograph the truck.Get the company name and DOT number on the cab door, the trailer number, the license plates, the damage and the road. These tell us exactly who to go after.
  3. Find witnesses.Get names and phone numbers of anyone who saw it happen, including other drivers who stopped.
  4. Get the crash report number.Ask the officer, whether it's local police, the county sheriff or the Illinois State Police, how to get a copy of the report.
  5. See a doctor ASAP.Gaps in treatment are the first thing insurers use to argue you weren't really hurt. Follow your doctor's plan and keep every appointment.
  6. Don't talk to the trucking company's people.Their adjusters and investigators work for the company. Don't give a recorded statement, sign anything or accept an offer before you talk to a lawyer.
  7. Call us right away.Driver logs, electronic logging data, dashcam video and the truck's own computer data can be lost or overwritten. We send a letter demanding the company keep it.

Know your rights

Laws and rules that affect a truck accident case

2 years

To file an injury claim

Most truck accident injury claims must be filed within 2 years of the crash. Claims against a town, county or other local government have a shorter, 1-year deadline.

Source735 ILCS 5/13-202Illinois Code of Civil Procedure ↗
11 hours

Driving limits for truckers

Federal rules generally limit a truck driver to 11 hours of driving after 10 hours off duty. Tired drivers who break these rules cause crashes.

Source49 CFR 395.3Federal Motor Carrier Safety Regulations ↗
$750K minimum

Required trucking insurance

Most for-hire trucks hauling freight across state lines must carry at least $750,000 in liability coverage. Hazardous loads require more.

Who is responsible

More than one company may owe you

In a car accident, there is usually one other driver and one insurance policy. In a truck accident, there can be several. Part of our job is finding every one of them.

  • The truck driver. Speeding, distraction, fatigue, impairment or breaking federal driving-hour rules.
  • The trucking company. For its driver, and for its own decisions about hiring, training, schedules and safety.
  • The truck or trailer owner. When the equipment belongs to a different company than the one running the load.
  • The shipper or loading company. Overloaded or poorly secured cargo can shift and cause a rollover or spill.
  • Maintenance shops and manufacturers. Bad brakes, worn tires and defective parts can all be someone else's responsibility.
Straight answers

Truck accident FAQ

How is a truck accident case different from a car accident case?

Truck cases usually involve more serious injuries, more than one company that may be responsible, federal safety rules, and larger insurance policies. The trucking company and its insurer also start building their defense right away, so it helps to have a lawyer involved early.

Who is responsible for a truck accident?

It may be the driver, the trucking company, the owner of the truck or trailer, the company that loaded the cargo, a maintenance shop or a parts maker. We investigate each one so no source of recovery is missed.

How long do trucking companies keep their records?

Federal rules require trucking companies to keep drivers' hours-of-service records and supporting documents for at least 6 months. Other evidence, like dashcam video and electronic data, can be overwritten much sooner. That's why we send a preservation letter as soon as you hire us.

What if the driver was an independent contractor?

That label doesn't end the question. The company whose name and DOT number are on the truck, the lease between the driver and the company, and who controlled the work can all make a company responsible. We sort that out for you.

What if I was partly at fault?

You can still recover in Illinois as long as you were 50% or less at fault. Your compensation is reduced by your share of the blame. Trucking insurers often try to shift blame to the car driver, and we push back with the evidence.

How much does it cost to hire you?

Nothing up front. We work on a contingency fee and are only paid if we recover money for you. If an insurer makes an offer before you hire us, we don't take a fee from that money.
