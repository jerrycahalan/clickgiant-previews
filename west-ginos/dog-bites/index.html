Illinois law is on your side after a dog bite

A dog bite can mean stitches, infections, nerve damage and scars on the face, arms and hands. For children, it can also mean months of fear. Medical bills add up fast, and many people don't know where to turn when the dog belongs to a neighbor, a friend or family.

Illinois doesn't give dogs a "free bite." If a dog attacks or injures you without provocation while you're somewhere you're allowed to be, the owner is responsible, even if the dog never hurt anyone before. In most cases, the claim is paid by the owner's homeowners or renters insurance, not out of their own pocket. Step by step

What to do after a dog bite in Illinois

A few simple steps protect your health and your claim.

  1. Get medical care the same day.Dog bites infect easily, even small ones. A doctor can clean the wound, check for nerve damage and decide whether you need a tetanus shot or rabies treatment.
  2. Find out who owns the dog.Get the owner's name, address and phone number, and the names of anyone who saw the attack.
  3. Report the bite.Call county animal control or the police. The report creates an official record, and the dog can be checked for rabies.
  4. Take photos.Photograph your injuries right away and as they heal, along with torn clothing and the place where it happened.
  5. Follow up with your doctor.Keep every appointment. Scarring, plastic surgery and counseling are all part of your claim.
  6. Don't give the insurer a recorded statement.The owner's insurance company may call quickly. Don't give a recorded statement or accept an offer before you talk to a lawyer.
  7. Call us for a free consultation.We'll tell you what your case may be worth and what happens next. There's no cost and no obligation.

Illinois dog bite laws that affect your case

To file an injury claim

Most dog bite injury claims must be filed within 2 years of the attack.

No free bite

Owner responsibility

If a dog attacks or injures someone without provocation, and the person was behaving peacefully somewhere they were allowed to be, the owner is responsible for the full injury.

Who counts as the owner

"Owner" includes the person who owns the dog, and also anyone who keeps it, harbors it, cares for it or knowingly lets it stay where they live.

Who pays your bills

Where the money comes from after a dog bite

Many people don't want to make a claim against someone they know. In most cases, they won't be paying out of pocket. Insurance will.

  • The owner's homeowners insurance. Most homeowners policies cover dog bites, wherever the attack happened.
  • The owner's renters insurance. Renters policies often include the same liability coverage.
  • A business's insurance. If the attack happened at a business or by a dog kept there, its liability coverage may apply.
  • Workers' compensation. If you were bitten while working, workers' comp may pay medical bills and lost wages too.
  • Your health insurance. It can cover treatment now, and we sort out repayment when the case settles.
Straight answers

Dog bite FAQ

Does the dog have to have bitten someone before?

No. Illinois has no "one bite" rule. The owner can be responsible the very first time the dog attacks, as long as the dog wasn't provoked and you were behaving peacefully somewhere you were allowed to be.

What counts as provoking a dog?

Provocation usually means doing something to the dog first, like hitting, teasing or hurting it. Even then, courts look at whether the dog's reaction was out of proportion. Don't assume you don't have a case. Ask us.

The dog belongs to a friend or relative. Will they have to pay?

Usually not. These claims are almost always paid by the owner's homeowners or renters insurance. Many of our clients stay on good terms with the owner throughout the case.

I wasn't bitten, but a dog knocked me down. Do I have a case?

Possibly. Illinois law covers dogs that attack, try to attack or injure someone, not just bites. Falls caused by a dog can lead to broken bones and serious injuries.

What if the bite happened on the owner's property?

You're covered if you were somewhere you had a right to be, like a guest, a delivery driver or a mail carrier. Trespassers generally can't use the dog bite law.

How much does it cost to hire you?
