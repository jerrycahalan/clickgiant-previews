Nursing home abuse & neglect · Central Illinois

Your loved one deserved better care

Families trust nursing homes with the people they love most. When a resident is neglected, left in bed until bedsores form, falls without help, goes without water or meals, or comes back with injuries no one can explain, that trust is broken.

The Illinois Nursing Home Care Act gives residents the right to be free from abuse and neglect, and it holds a home's owner and licensee responsible for injuries caused by their staff. On these cases we partner with experienced co-counsel and stay involved, so you get the right lawyer for your claim and a familiar face to call.

If someone is in danger right now, call 911. For anything else, call us. We'll listen, help you report it and explain your options for free.

Warning signs

Signs of nursing home neglect and abuse

Bedsores

Pressure sores that start or get worse can mean a resident isn't being turned, cleaned or checked often enough.

Falls & fractures

Repeated falls, broken hips or head injuries, especially when the resident needed help to move safely.

Weight loss & dehydration

Sudden weight loss, dry mouth or confusion can mean meals and water are being missed.

Unexplained injuries

Bruises, cuts or burns with no good explanation, or stories that don't add up.

Changes in mood

A resident who becomes withdrawn, fearful or anxious around certain staff members.

Medication problems

Missed doses, wrong medications or a resident who seems overly sedated.

Step by step

What to do if you suspect nursing home abuse

You don't need proof to speak up. These steps protect your loved one first.

  1. If there's immediate danger, call 911.Your loved one's safety comes before anything else.
  2. Report it to the state.Call the Illinois Department of Public Health Nursing Home Hotline at 1-800-252-4343, available 24 hours a day, or file online through IDPH.
  3. Contact the Long-Term Care Ombudsman.The free Ombudsman program advocates for residents and helps families with concerns.
  4. Document what you see.Take photos of injuries and conditions, write down dates, names and what staff told you, and keep copies of any records.
  5. Get a medical check.Ask for an outside doctor to examine your loved one and record any injuries.
  6. Call us for a free consultation.We'll explain your loved one's rights under Illinois law and what a claim would involve.
Know your rights

The Illinois Nursing Home Care Act

Free from abuse

Residents' rights

Every nursing home resident in Illinois has the right to be free from abuse and neglect.

Source210 ILCS 45/2-107Nursing Home Care Act, per Ill. Supreme Court ↗
Owner liable

Responsible for staff

The owner and licensee are liable to a resident for any intentional or negligent act or omission of their agents or employees that injures the resident.

Source210 ILCS 45/3-601Nursing Home Care Act, per Ill. Supreme Court ↗
Fees paid

Attorney fees for residents

When a resident wins, the licensee must pay actual damages, costs and attorney fees. That helps families bring cases even when the money involved is small.

Source210 ILCS 45/3-602Nursing Home Care Act, per Ill. Supreme Court ↗

Where to report: the IDPH Nursing Home Hotline (1-800-252-4343, 24 hours) and the Illinois Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program.

Straight answers

Nursing home abuse FAQ

What counts as nursing home neglect?

Neglect is when a home fails to give the care a resident needs, such as turning a bedbound resident, helping with walking and toileting, giving food, water and medication, or getting medical help when something is wrong. Bedsores, falls and dehydration are common signs.

Who do I report nursing home abuse to in Illinois?

Call the Illinois Department of Public Health Nursing Home Hotline at 1-800-252-4343, available 24 hours a day. You can also contact the Long-Term Care Ombudsman. If someone is in immediate danger, call 911.

Will reporting it make things worse for my loved one?

Residents have the right to be free from abuse and neglect, and reporting helps protect them and other residents. If you're worried about retaliation, tell us and the Ombudsman, and we'll talk through options such as moving your loved one. Trent/Michael to confirm

Can the nursing home be held responsible for what an employee did?

Yes. Under the Nursing Home Care Act, the owner and licensee are liable for intentional or negligent acts of their agents or employees that injure a resident.

My loved one passed away. Can our family still bring a claim?

Possibly. Families may have claims after a resident dies because of neglect. We'll explain who can file and the deadlines that apply.

How much does it cost?

Nothing up front. The Nursing Home Care Act also requires the home to pay attorney fees when a resident wins.
