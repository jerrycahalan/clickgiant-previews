You don't have to figure out probate alone

Probate is the court process for settling someone's estate after they die: proving the will, paying valid debts and passing property to the right people. If you've been named executor, or you're the closest family member, it can feel like a lot to take on while you're grieving.

Not every estate needs a full probate case. Smaller estates may be settled with a small estate affidavit, and many estates can be handled through independent administration, with less court involvement. We'll tell you which path fits and handle the paperwork with you.

Step by step

How Illinois probate works

Here's the usual path. We'll walk you through each step.

  1. File the will.Whoever has the original will should file it with the clerk of the circuit court right away.
  2. Find out what probate is needed.Some estates qualify for a small estate affidavit. Others need a court case.
  3. Get appointed by the court.The judge appoints the executor or administrator and issues letters of office, which give them authority to act.
  4. Notify heirs and creditors.Heirs get notice, and a notice to creditors runs in a local newspaper.
  5. Gather assets and pay valid bills.Collect accounts and property, and pay valid debts and expenses from the estate.
  6. Distribute what's left.Property goes to the people named in the will, or to the heirs under Illinois law if there's no will.
  7. Close the estate.Final paperwork is filed and the estate is closed.

Know your rights

Illinois probate rules

$150K or less

Small estate affidavit

When a person's personal property is worth $150,000 or less, not counting registered vehicles, it may be collected with a small estate affidavit instead of a full probate case.

Source755 ILCS 5/25-1Probate Act of 1975 ↗
6 months

For creditors to file claims

The estate publishes notice once a week for 3 weeks. Creditors then have at least 6 months from the first notice, or 3 months after a mailed notice, whichever is later.

Source755 ILCS 5/18-3Probate Act of 1975 ↗
Less court time

Independent administration

Independent administration lets the executor or administrator handle most of the estate without court orders, unless the law requires one or an interested person asks for it.

Source755 ILCS 5/28-1Probate Act of 1975 ↗
Before your first meeting

What to bring

Bring what you can find. We'll help you track down the rest.

  • The death certificate. A copy is fine to start.
  • The original will, if there is one. Don't write on it or remove staples.
  • A list of assets and accounts. Bank statements, retirement accounts, life insurance and vehicles.
  • Deeds and titles. For any real estate or vehicles.
  • Names and addresses of family members. Including the people named in the will.
Straight answers

Probate FAQ

Does every estate have to go through probate?

No. Property owned jointly or with a named beneficiary often passes outside probate, and smaller estates may use a small estate affidavit. We'll look at what your loved one owned and tell you what's needed. Trent to confirm

How long does probate take in Illinois?

At least 6 months, because creditors have at least that long to file claims after notice is published. Many estates take longer, depending on property, debts and whether there are disagreements.

What if there's no will?

The court appoints an administrator, and Illinois law decides who inherits. For example, with a spouse and children, the spouse receives half and the children share the other half.

Do I have to pay my loved one's debts myself?

Generally, debts are paid from the estate, not from your own pocket. Call us before paying any bills so we can make sure it's done in the right order. Trent to confirm
