Make the decisions now, so your family doesn't have to guess

Most families put this off. But without a will, Illinois law decides who gets what, and the split may not match what you'd want. Without powers of attorney, your family may have to go to court just to pay your bills or talk with your doctors if you can't.

An estate plan lets you choose who receives your property, who handles your estate, who would raise your children and who makes decisions if you can't. We keep it simple and explain every document before you sign.

Step by step

How estate planning works with us

Most plans take just a couple of meetings.

  1. Call to set a time.Meet at either office, or we can come to you.
  2. Make a simple list.What you own, your bank and retirement accounts, life insurance and who is named as beneficiary.
  3. Think about your people.Who should handle your estate, who would raise your children, and who you trust to make decisions for you.
  4. We draft your documents.We explain each one in plain English and answer every question.
  5. Sign with witnesses.We handle the signing at our office so everything is done right.
  6. Keep the originals safe.Tell the person handling your estate where to find them.
  7. Review after big changes.Marriage, divorce, a new child or grandchild, a move or a death in the family are all good times to update your plan.

Know your rights

Illinois estate planning laws

2 witnesses

What makes a will valid

An Illinois will must be in writing, signed by you (or by someone at your direction, in your presence), and witnessed in your presence by 2 or more credible witnesses.

Source755 ILCS 5/4-3Probate Act of 1975 ↗
½ to your spouse

If there's no will

If you die without a will and leave a spouse and children, your spouse receives half of your estate and your children share the other half.

Source755 ILCS 5/2-1Probate Act of 1975 ↗
1 trusted agent

Health care decisions

A health care power of attorney lets your agent make medical decisions for you, either right away or only when you can no longer make them yourself, depending on what you choose.

Source755 ILCS 45/4-10Illinois Power of Attorney Act ↗
Before your first meeting

What to bring

Don't worry if you don't have everything. We'll help you fill in the gaps.

  • A list of what you own. Your home, land, vehicles and anything of special value.
  • Account information. Bank, retirement and investment accounts.
  • Life insurance and beneficiary forms. These often pass outside your will, so we want to see who is named.
  • Names of the people you trust. For executor, guardian and power of attorney.
  • Any existing will or power of attorney. So we can update rather than start over.
Straight answers

Estate planning FAQ

Do I need a will if I don't have much?

Yes. A will isn't only about money. It lets you name who handles your estate and who would raise your children, and it keeps the state's default rules from deciding for you.

What happens if I die without a will in Illinois?

Illinois law decides who inherits. For example, if you leave a spouse and children, your spouse receives half and your children share the other half, even if that's not what you would have chosen.

Why do I need a power of attorney?

If an illness or accident leaves you unable to act, a power of attorney lets someone you choose pay your bills and make medical decisions. Without one, your family may need to go to court to get that authority. Trent to confirm

Can I change my will later?

Yes. You can update your will and powers of attorney anytime. We recommend a review after big life changes.
